# Prince of Tides: Brand Voice

Register: quiet luxury. Understated, literate, precise, calm, a little wry. The luxury is in the
detail, never in the adjectives.

## Positioning statement
For couples, small families and friends who would rather have a calm, well-kept base than a
high-rise view, Prince of Tides is an updated two-bedroom, two-bath condo inside a 24-hour gated
resort on Hilton Head Island. The heated indoor pool, sauna and steam room are a few steps from the
door. The beach is reached by a boardwalk over the salt marsh. It is as good on a grey February
afternoon as on a bright morning in June.

## Who we are talking to
- **Couples**: a king bed with its own bath, a steam room down the corridor, nowhere to be.
- **Small families (up to 6)**: two bedrooms, two full baths, a travel crib, a kiddie pool, a gate.
- **Friends**: separate rooms, two bathrooms, lit courts, a beach bar at the end of the boardwalk.
- **Snowbirds and remote workers (shoulder season, winter)**: Wi-Fi, an indoor pool and walking
  track under one roof, mid-island errands within minutes.

## Voice
| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Understated, assured, a little wry | Excited, breathless, salesy |
| Precise (831 sq ft, 4:00 PM, 0.8 mi) | Vague ("close to everything") |
| Candid about trade-offs, without apology | Evasive, or defensive |
| Observational: low tide, planks, steam | Stock adjectives |
| Literate, plain, short | Clever for its own sake |

### Rules
1. No exclamation points in our own copy. (Guest reviews are quoted as written.)
2. No superlatives we cannot prove. No hype.
3. Let one specific detail do the work an adjective would.
4. Short declarative sentences. Calm rhythm. Some fragments are fine.
5. Editorial structure: sections carry numbered titles ("I. The Rooms").
6. Trust signals are stated as plain facts ("Guest rating 9.2 / 10 on Vrbo"), not chips.
7. CTAs are quiet and direct: "Check dates", "Request a stay".
8. American English. Never the em dash: use commas, periods, colons or parentheses.

## Tagline options
1. "Rest, at the pace of the tide." **(Pick)**
2. "A quiet residence, mid-island."
3. "Kept by the tide. Mid-island, Hilton Head."

**Why #1:** it names the actual offer (rest, slowness), echoes the name without leaning on it, and
makes no claim about views or the beach that a guest could dispute.

## Words to use
quiet, kept, considered, unhurried, marsh, boardwalk, low tide, gated, heated, steam, particulars,
residence, mid-island, request, a stay, check dates.

## Words to avoid
nestled, oasis, paradise, look no further, hidden gem, unlock, seamless, elevate, reimagine,
curated, luxurious, stunning, breathtaking, world-class, perfect, amazing, oceanfront, oceanview,
beachfront, steps to the beach, and any claim we cannot verify.

## The name
"Prince of Tides" nods to the Lowcountry: tidal marsh, slow water, a storytelling coast. A light
sovereign note is permitted, once, and wryly. We do not quote any novel or film, borrow its
imagery, or imply any affiliation.

## Framing the honest trade-offs
State the trade-off once, plainly, then the thing gained. No apology, no fine print.

**The beach is a walk.** "The beach is 0.8 to 1.1 miles away, by a raised boardwalk over the salt
marsh. In season, a complimentary shuttle. Chairs are in your locker." Never "steps to the beach".

**The windows face the parking lot and the indoor pool atrium.** "There is no ocean view. There is
a heated pool, a sauna and a steam room a few steps from the door, which in February is the better
arrangement." Never "views" or "scenic".

**No in-unit laundry, no pantry basics, warm bedrooms, 4:00 PM check-in, parking passes, door
codes, an HOA elevator.** Handled in "Before You Arrive" as matter-of-fact notes. Settled
expectations protect reviews.
